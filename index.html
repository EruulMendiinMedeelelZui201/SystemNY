WELLNESS DATABASE // 001

THE ART OF REST

A practical, evidence-informed guide to better sleep, nighttime overthinking, and mental recovery.

🌙

FIRST, A LITTLE REMINDER

You don't have to solve every problem before going to sleep. You don't have to figure out your entire future tonight. Rest is not something you need to earn by being productive. Your mind deserves a break, even when life feels complicated.

01 // ROUTINE

Build a consistent sleep schedule

Your body has an internal clock called the circadian rhythm. Keeping a relatively consistent bedtime and wake-up time helps regulate it.

  • Wake up at approximately the same time every day.
  • Teenagers generally need 8–10 hours of sleep.
  • Avoid dramatically changing your sleep schedule on weekends.
  • Create a relaxing routine before bedtime.
02 // ENVIRONMENT

Design your sleep environment

Your bedroom should help your brain associate the environment with relaxation and rest.

  • Keep the room comfortably cool, quiet, and dark.
  • Use comfortable bedding and pillows.
  • Reduce unnecessary noise and bright lighting.
  • Keep stressful activities away from your bed when possible.
03 // DIGITAL HABITS

Give your brain a digital break

Endless scrolling and stimulating content can make it harder to transition into a relaxed state.

  • Try putting your phone away 30–60 minutes before bed.
  • Lower screen brightness in the evening.
  • Avoid emotionally stressful conversations right before sleeping.
  • Replace scrolling with reading, calm music, or journaling.
04 // MENTAL WELLNESS

Quiet the nighttime overthinking

Sometimes the hardest part of sleeping isn't feeling tired. It's having a mind that refuses to slow down.

  • Write down worries instead of repeatedly thinking about them.
  • Make a short list of things you can deal with tomorrow.
  • Try slow, comfortable breathing without forcing it.
  • Remind yourself that nighttime is not the time to solve everything.
"Not every thought deserves your attention at 2 AM."
05 // DAILY HABITS

Support your natural sleep cycle

  • Get some natural daylight in the morning.
  • Include enjoyable physical activity during the day.
  • Avoid caffeine late in the day, especially energy drinks.
  • Avoid heavy meals immediately before bedtime if they make you uncomfortable.
  • If you nap, keep it relatively short and avoid late-evening naps.
06 // WHEN SLEEP DOESN'T COME

Don't turn bedtime into a battle

If you cannot fall asleep and become increasingly frustrated, don't force yourself to remain in bed indefinitely.

  • Try a quiet, relaxing activity in dim lighting.
  • Return to bed when you feel sleepy again.
  • Avoid repeatedly checking the time.
  • Keep your wake-up time reasonably consistent.

WHEN TO SEEK EXTRA SUPPORT

Occasional sleepless nights happen. But if difficulty sleeping continues, affects daily life, or leaves you constantly exhausted, consider talking with a healthcare professional.

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) is an established treatment for persistent insomnia. A healthcare professional can help determine whether it is appropriate.

If family stress or emotional difficulties are affecting your sleep, talking with a trusted person, school counselor, or mental health professional can also help. You don't have to manage everything alone.
